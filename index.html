The Hierarchy of Teaching Terminology

🧭 4 levels · 20 MCQs

Understanding the relationship between an Approach, Strategy, Method and Technique is key to effective lesson planning and delivery in TEFL. They form a top-down hierarchy, from broad philosophy to specific classroom actions.

📌 Source: Supervision of English language, Kafr El-Sheikh · Developed by Dr. Mazen Badawy – Doctorate of English Teaching & Testing.

Approach (Why)
Strategy (How – broadly)
Method (What)
Technique
(How – specifically)

Open each level for its meaning and TEFL examples

01 🔴 Approach — the “Why”

Focus level: Philosophical / theoretical.

The overall theory or set of beliefs about the nature of language and how it is learned. It is the “why” behind your teaching.

  • Communicative Language Teaching (CLT): focuses on meaningful communication.
  • Grammar-Translation Approach: emphasises grammatical accuracy and translation.
  • Audiolingual Approach: based on habit formation through repetition and drills.
  • Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT): views language as a tool to complete real-world tasks.
  • Lexical Approach: centres on vocabulary and lexical chunks.
02 🟠 Strategy — the “How”, broadly

Focus level: Planning / framework.

A general, long-term plan based on your chosen approach. It guides how you will structure and deliver content.

  • Cooperative Learning: students work together in groups to maximise their own and each other’s learning.
  • Problem-Based Learning: students learn by working collaboratively to solve a complex, open-ended problem.
  • Inquiry-Based Learning: investigation and asking questions to discover knowledge.
  • Learning through Play: games and fun activities that support learning and engagement.
  • Integrated Skills Strategy: blending reading, writing, listening and speaking in one lesson.
03 🟢 Method — the “What”

Focus level: Procedure / structure.

A specific, systematic and structured procedure for delivering a lesson, consistent with your strategy.

  • The Lecture Method: the teacher presents information, often with interactive elements.
  • The Discussion Method: teacher-facilitated conversation where students explore a topic and build understanding.
  • Project-Based Method: an extended project or field trip to apply language skills practically.
  • Presentation–Practice–Production (PPP): a common structure for introducing new language.
  • Total Physical Response (TPR): students respond to commands with physical actions.
04 🔵 Technique — the “How”, specifically

Focus level: Action / tool.

The specific, individual actions, tools or activities you use in the classroom to implement your method.

  • Brainstorming: generating ideas collectively about a topic.
  • Role-plays and information-gap activities.
  • Chants and stories: making language memorable and fun, especially for young learners.
  • Skill of questioning: introductory, developing and evaluative questions.
  • Visuals and non-verbal communication.
  • Think-Pair-Share in speaking practice.
  • Drills and repetition.

✅ Effective classroom practices

  • Connect with your students personally.
  • Create a comfortable and safe learning environment.
  • Establish a clear daily routine.
  • Speak slowly and pronounce words clearly.
  • Focus on visual elements in lessons.

🌟 Keep building on them

  • Check for comprehension frequently.
  • Provide interactive and engaging lessons.
  • Learn about students’ cultural backgrounds.
  • Integrate technology into lessons.
  • Make learning English a fun experience.

Session One · Classroom practice

Strategies You Should Know About Teaching English

🧭 14 strategies

How? Build the lesson around the learner: connect first, lower the fear, make the language visible and playful, and check understanding before moving on. Open each strategy for the full guidance.

01 🤝 Connect with your students

If you imagine yourself as a student sitting with a stranger in class who speaks a foreign language that you find difficult to understand, you can understand how scary it can be when the teacher launches straight into his lesson without an ice-breaker activity.

02 🛡️ Create a comfortable and safe learning environment

Students need to feel safe to express themselves fully. By creating a positive learning environment—not only physically but also emotionally—students will be more willing to try new things and, most importantly, practise their English skills. Correct their mistakes gently.

03 🗓️ Establish a daily routine schedule in your class

Writing a daily agenda, allocating a specific time for group sessions, or assigning an activity for students when they enter the classroom will help them figure out their lesson plans.

Make sure to share clear objectives. Using examples is also a great way to bridge the gap between communicating objectives and student understanding.

04 🐢 Speak slowly and pronounce the words

Slow down your speaking speed so that everyone in the class has a chance to hear every word you say.

Speak louder than usual at times: it is important for students to learn how to pronounce vocabulary and hear all the nuances of the word.

05 🙌 Use non-verbal communication

For example, show each preposition by acting it out. When you say “in,” act by putting something in your mouth and repeating the movements—repetition helps children remember.

Note: the more unusual the verb or action is, the easier it will be for students to remember later.

06 👀 Focus on visual and visual elements in lessons

Make learning English fun and interesting so that it becomes more sticky and ingrained in the minds of your students.

📌 Phrase kept as written in the source.

07 ✅ Check comprehension

Most language learners are shy and reluctant to admit in front of the whole class that they do not understand something—and you would be surprised how many students pretend they understood when they have no idea what you are talking about.

Pause after explaining something or giving instructions to check understanding. Ask more questions or explain the context a little more before you move on.

08 🔄 Be adaptable

Note: some people find it wise not to use the phrase “Do you understand?” in a non-native English speaking classroom, because students are often eager to please their teachers and will always answer this question in the affirmative.

09 🎲 Provide interactive lessons

Make your lessons more engaging and interactive. Using visuals, activities and infographics can help students with their learning.

You can also teach the same concept multiple times using different strategies, such as singing, videos, actions and stories.

10 🔤 Explain new vocabulary before using it

Explain new vocabulary to your students before using it in lessons. The goal is that learning vocabulary first will help your students focus on the overall learning objectives of the lesson.

11 🧩 Use various teaching strategies

Tailor your lessons to meet the individual needs of your students.

Differentiation in teaching goes much further than having high, medium and low level worksheets and activities:

  • Level outcomes and activities based on students’ ability.
  • Use lots of hands-on learning activities.
  • Provide students with choice in their learning—this increases their engagement in class.
12 🌍 Learn about students’ cultural backgrounds

Students need to retain recently acquired English vocabulary by linking it to their real-life experiences, so it is stored in their long-term memory.

13 💻 Use technology

We live in a digital world dominated by technology tools and applications. Introducing and integrating digital tools and technologies into the curriculum and lesson plans is a great way to reach digitally savvy students and engage them in more creative ways.

14 🎉 Make language a fun factor

Make language a fun factor: let humour, play and enjoyable tasks be part of the lesson so pupils look forward to English.

⏱️ ~6 min

Takeaway: the strategies work as a system—safety enables participation, participation enables practice, and practice builds confidence.

Session One · Methods

What Are the Methods of Teaching English?

🏫 Primary · Middle · Secondary

There are many methods listed by expert teachers specialised in teaching English, and each method is given to a different stage, including primary, middle and secondary.

🧱 How to teach English to pupils

To teach a pupil English, follow important basics that give the student rules for the language, then progress with him to the higher levels until the summit is reached.

🧒 Why new methods are needed

Pupils are among the age groups that are difficult to deal with and to introduce English information to. New methods were issued to make this process easier for both the teacher and the student.

🧑‍🏫 How do I teach English?

🖼️

1 · Picture ↔ word

Give pupils the skill of matching the picture with the word after learning it.

🔠

2 · Letters and pictures

Master the pronunciation of English letters, then insert a picture on each letter and connect it with the child.

✍️

3 · Letter → word

Let the student place a picture on each letter and write a word that begins with that letter.

🎧

4 · Audio clips

Provide audio clips that teach the correct pronunciation method.

🃏

5 · Fun games

Introduce fun games, including arranging cards.

🗣️ How to get students to use English in class

Activate pupils’ minds and bodies, attract their attention, and stay away from everything common and disliked. These methods help you become the teacher pupils look forward to.

🔤

Interesting letters

Teach letters in an interesting way, such as showing a picture with each letter.

🔢

Numbers with objects

Address numbers while bringing something intangible and tangible that refers to the number.

🃏

Student-delivered flashcards

Use flashcards that are delivered by the student.

📝

Simple → medium → sentence

Start with simple words, move to medium ones, then coordinate them into sentences.

Takeaway: methods must fit the stage and the age group—young learners need concrete, visual and playful routes into English.

Session Two · Key distinction

Teaching Method vs. Teaching Style

🔍 Compare

The two terms are related but not the same: a method is the general approach used in a teaching situation, while a style is the teacher’s own characteristic way of carrying it out.

🧭

Teaching method

  • It is a general style adopted by teachers.
  • This takes place in a specific educational situation.
  • Each teacher implements the method of teaching English in his own style, which is distinct from others.
✍️

Teaching style

  • It is a behaviour that each teacher adopts.
  • It becomes the teacher’s own characteristic.
  • No teacher’s style can be similar to another teacher’s style—each teacher has his own.
🔑 Quick test: “What approach are we using?” points to method. “How does this particular teacher bring it to life?” points to style.
📋 Section evaluation10 MCQs

Separate general method from personal style in definitions and classroom scenarios.

⏱️ ~4 min
💡 Model answer & takeaway

Model answer: a teaching method is a general style adopted by teachers in a specific educational situation. A teaching style is a behaviour that becomes the teacher’s own characteristic. Each teacher applies a method in a distinct style, and no two styles are identical.

Takeaway: methods can be shared; style is personal. Effective teachers know the method and refine their own style.

Session Two · Six methods

Modern Methods Used in Teaching English

🧩 6 methods

Six methods that can shape an English lesson—from the oldest and most widespread to cooperative and playful approaches.

01 🎤 Lecture

It is considered one of the oldest teaching methods, and also the most widespread and used among teachers. It is one of the old traditional methods of teaching.

It can be a modern method by adding kinetic and attractive means that make learning English easier, using the motor senses and group motor activity within the lecture.

This old method relies entirely on the teacher: the teacher presents information to the student in an explanatory or narrative form, and the student is only the recipient.

02 💬 Discussion

This method is considered a modern method in which the teacher delivers information by presenting a group discussion with students on a specific topic in English.

The discussion takes place with the participation of all students; the teacher supervises and guides them. The teacher gives students different sources so they get basic information before the discussion, then students arrive at final information through reasoning and inference.

The discussion method develops students’ linguistic learning and English speaking skills—unlike the traditional method, which presents information only in a rote way, which is completely rejected in language learning.

03 🚌 Group work project or field trip

Students carry out extensive social activities or social trips in which they interact and mingle with English-speaking tourist groups, guide them and talk to them by setting up special camps.

Youth trips develop language skills through practical and actual application during the trip.

This method is considered one of the most important modern methods used in teaching English.

04 🧠 Problem solving

In this method, the teacher asks questions about a specific thing in the language to make students think about solving the problem.

It develops research, experimentation, questioning and other skills, alongside modern educational methods.

The teacher supervises the process and paves the way for the correct answer by giving students a glimpse of it.

05 🎵 Chants and stories

This method is suitable for some educational topics, but not all.

It is usually followed for the first three grades because it is fun and encourages the student to study: the student learns the language in a fun, enjoyable and very easy way.

It is described as the closest way to quickly teaching English while building a love for it.

06 🤝 Cooperative learning

The teacher divides the class into groups, and each group searches for a specific linguistic problem or topic.

After that, each group shares what it has learned with the other group.

The teacher’s role is to follow up and organise the group work; he does not participate in any way until the students learn group work.

Takeaway: no method is universally best—match the method to the age group, the topic and the learning goal.

Session Two · Active speaking

Learning Through Play

🎲 5 steps

A guessing-game activity that turns the class into a short play. Pupils practise descriptions, yes/no questions and speaking—without realising how much English they are using.

👥

1 · Divide

Pupils are divided into groups.

💭

2 · Choose a character

Each group thinks of a character.

🎙️

3 · Give a clue

One pupil comes in front of the class and starts the play by providing some information, for example: “She isn’t a doctor. But she works in hospital.”

❓

4 · Ask questions

The pupils ask yes/no questions, such as: “Is he the manager?” “Does she help the doctor?”

🎉

5 · Guess

“Yes, she is a nurse…”

🗣️ Language focus: descriptions · jobs · yes/no questions · short answers

Model answer: groups → choose a character → one pupil gives a clue in front of the class → classmates ask yes/no questions → the class guesses the character (“Yes, she is a nurse”).

Takeaway: the activity is simple to set up and gives every pupil a reason to listen, question and speak.

Skill of Questioning

❓ 4 types · 3 levels

Questions are the teacher’s most flexible tool. Use different types to move a lesson forward and different levels to match the thinking you want to develop.

📋 Types of questions

  • Introductory or preliminary questions — open the topic, warm learners up and connect to prior knowledge.
  • Developing questions — probe, extend and deepen an answer.
  • Evaluating or testing questions — check understanding and assess learning.
  • Recapitulatory questions — recall, summarise and consolidate what was learned.

🪜 Levels of questions

  • Lower order level — recall and basic understanding (name, list, identify).
  • Middle order level — application and explanation (use, compare, explain).
  • Higher order level — analysis, evaluation and creation (analyse, judge, design).

📌 The source lists the categories and levels. The short definitions above explain how each is used in classroom practice.

🚪

Introductory

Open the lesson and activate prior knowledge.

🔎

Developing

Follow up with “Why?”, “How do you know?”.

📊

Evaluating

Check whether the learning goal has been met.

🔁

Recapitulatory

Summarise the main points before closing.

📋 Section evaluation10 MCQs

Classify question types and levels, then choose the right question for a purpose.

⏱️ ~5 min
💡 Model answer & takeaway

Model answer: the four types are introductory/preliminary, developing, evaluating/testing and recapitulatory. The three levels are lower order, middle order and higher order.

Takeaway: plan a question sequence—open, develop, check, summarise—and deliberately include higher-order thinking, not only recall.

Skill of Whiteboard Writing

🖍️ 3 criteria

The board is a teaching tool, not a notebook. Write so that every pupil can read it from the back of the room, keep the layout tidy, and make the content appropriate for the lesson.

👁️ I. Legibility of handwriting

  • Adequate spacing between two letters
  • Adequate spacing between two words
  • The size of the letter is large enough to be read from the far end of the room
  • The size of the capital letter is just bigger than the small letter
  • All the capital letters are the same size
  • All the small letters are of the same size

📐 Neatness in whiteboard writing

  • The words and sentences written are parallel to the base of the blackboard
  • Adequate spacing between the lines
  • Retention of relevant matter on the blackboard

✅ Appropriateness of written work

  • Continuity in the points
  • Brief and simple points
  • Underlining the important points
  • Use of colour chalk pieces
  • Diagrams drawn
  • Simple, large, clear and proportionate size diagrams
📋 Section evaluation10 MCQs

Distinguish legibility, neatness and appropriateness in board writing.

⏱️ ~5 min
💡 Model answer & takeaway

Model answer: legibility = letter/word spacing, readable size, consistent capital and small letter sizes. Neatness = straight lines parallel to the board, adequate line spacing, only relevant material retained. Appropriateness = logical continuity, brief points, underlining, colour, and clear, proportionate diagrams.

Takeaway: before writing, ask: can the back row read it, is the layout clean, and does every item deserve board space?

Skill of Achieving Closure

🧠 Consolidate

Closure is how a lesson ends with meaning. It pulls the main points together, connects them to what pupils already know, and prepares them to use the learning beyond the lesson.

📌

1 · Consolidate the major points

Questions and statements by the teacher related to the consolidation of the major points covered during the lesson.

⏪

2 · Link to past knowledge

Opportunities provided by the teacher for pupils to link the present knowledge with their past knowledge.

🌍

3 · Apply to new situations

Opportunities provided by the teacher for pupils to apply the knowledge gained during the lesson to new situations.

⏩

4 · Link to future learning

Opportunities provided by the teacher for pupils to link the present knowledge with their future learning.

⏱️ Remember: closure is not only “Any questions?”. It is a planned moment of consolidation, connection and transfer.

📋 Section evaluation10 MCQs

Recognise closure moves and plan an ending that consolidates, links and transfers learning.

⏱️ ~4 min
💡 Model answer & takeaway

Model answer: closure includes teacher questions/statements that consolidate the major points, opportunities to link present knowledge with past knowledge, opportunities to apply learning to new situations, and opportunities to link present learning with future learning.

Takeaway: plan the last five minutes as carefully as the first five—closure is where learning is organised and carried forward.

Lesson Preparation

🧠 2 types · 6 steps

Planning for any work is a secret to its success. Lesson preparation is planning, first mentally and then in writing.

Types of preparation

🧠 Mental preparation

Reading, understanding and mentally analysing the scientific material before writing anything down. This is essential.

✍️ Written preparation

Writing down the lesson plan with specific objectives, procedures and evaluation methods. This follows mental preparation.

Preparation steps — open each one

01 📖 Read the lesson

Read the lesson to be taught well and analyse its elements: ideas, facts and concepts.

02 📚 Use sources

Use appropriate sources to enrich the material and clarify any confusing issues.

03 🖼️ Form a mental image

This is the actual value of preparation and it is indispensable.

04 🎯 Formulate measurable objectives

Write objectives that can be measured (for example enumerate, explain, conclude) and that suit all student levels.

05 ⏱️ Plan time management

Write down the approximate time required for each objective.

06 🧩 Mention methods

Preparation should include the names of the teaching methods you plan to use.

Benefits of preparation

  • The teacher organises ideas well.
  • Leads to accuracy in teaching and good planning.
  • Increases the teacher’s self-confidence.
  • The teacher has good control over the class.
  • Increases the student’s confidence in the teacher.
  • Helps in tracking curriculum progress and achieved objectives.
  • Uncovers hidden details of the subject matter.

Lesson planning · Explanatory guide

Core Steps in Lesson Planning & Their Significance

🗂️ 8 steps

Every step of a plan has a definition, a classroom example and a significance. Open each step to read all three.

01 🎯 Learning objectives

Definition: formulating clear, measurable targets (SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) that state what learners will know or do by the end of the lesson.

Example: “By the end of the lesson, students will be able to write 5 sentences expressing environmental solutions using modal verbs (should, must) with 85% accuracy.”

Significance: gives teacher and students clear direction and serves as the benchmark against which assessment and learning success are measured.

02 🧭 Teaching strategies

Definition: selecting instructional approaches and methodologies (direct instruction, inquiry-based, scaffolded learning, collaborative work) tailored to learner needs.

Example: Think-Pair-Share for brainstorm warm-ups and Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) for peer problem-solving.

Significance: accommodates diverse learning styles and mixed abilities, ensuring optimal engagement and comprehension.

03 🎒 Resources & learning materials

Definition: identifying and preparing the visual, auditory, digital or physical aids needed to support delivery.

Example: interactive whiteboard graphics, authentic audio clips, vocabulary flashcards, realia and differentiated worksheets.

Significance: enhances clarity, keeps learners engaged and makes abstract language concrete.

04 🧱 Presentation (explicit instruction)

Definition: the explicit delivery of new content, broken into digestible visual and conceptual parts through targeted scaffolding.

A. Vocabulary presentation — convey meaning, pronunciation and form (MPF) using synonyms, antonyms, examples, context clues, flashcards and realia, without relying on translation.

Example: present “sustainability” with an environmental infographic, flashcards of solar panels, concept-checking questions (CCQs) and by eliciting the antonym “depletion”.

B. Structure / language expressions — introduce grammar patterns inductively (guided discovery) or deductively (explicit rule) in communicative contexts.

Example: conditionals for advice: “If we recycle plastic, we can protect oceans.”

Significance: anchors new input securely, prevents confusion and lays the foundation for practice.

05 🏋️ Practice (guided & independent)

Definition: moving students from controlled, scaffolded tasks to autonomous, creative language production.

Example: Guided — a sentence-completion worksheet. Independent — a role-played debate on community recycling policies.

Significance: builds fluency, reinforces memory and shifts the class from teacher-centred to student-centred.

06 📝 Assessment

Definition: continuous evaluation during and after instruction — formative (questions, checks for understanding) and summative (quizzes, exit tickets).

Example: exit ticket: “Write 2 sentences using today’s structure to recommend a solution to air pollution.”

Significance: shows whether objectives were met, allowing real-time adjustment and data-driven planning.

07 💬 Constructive feedback

Definition: timely, actionable guidance on learners’ strengths and areas to improve during activities.

Example: “Great job applying ‘must’! After ‘must’ the verb stays in its base form—say ‘must protect’, not ‘must protecting’.”

Significance: corrects misconceptions early, boosts confidence and promotes self-regulation.

08 🏠 Assignment / follow-up

Definition: relevant homework or extension activities that consolidate classroom learning.

Example: “Write a 100-word paragraph proposing 3 eco-friendly habits for your household using 4 new vocabulary words and 2 conditional structures.”

Significance: extends learning beyond the classroom, reinforces retention and prepares for the next concepts.

📋 Section evaluation10 MCQs

Match each classroom example to its planning step and recall why the step matters.

⏱️ ~6 min
💡 Model answer & takeaway

Model answer: objectives → strategies → resources → presentation (vocabulary and structure) → practice (guided then independent) → assessment → feedback → assignment.

Takeaway: each step feeds the next—objectives decide what to assess, and assessment tells you what to plan tomorrow.

Lesson planning · Worked example

Actual Executable Lesson Plan

📋 60 minutes

A complete lesson that integrates all eight steps and the methodologies above. Open each step to see exactly what the teacher and learners do.

🎓 Target audience

Intermediate (B1/B2) ESL/EFL students

⏱️ Duration

60 minutes

🌍 Topic

Environmental Sustainability & Eco-Solutions

🧩 Integrated skills

Reading, speaking, grammar and vocabulary

01 🎯 Learning objectives (SMART)

Time: pre-lesson planning. By the end of this 60-minute lesson, students will be able to:

  • Vocabulary: identify and use 4 key environmental terms (sustainability, renewable, conservation, deplete) in context using synonyms, antonyms and context clues with 80% accuracy.
  • Reading: apply extensive reading to extract the main idea and intensive reading to scan for specific factual details.
  • Grammar & function: formulate 4 sentences using First Conditionals (If + Present Simple, will/can + base verb) to express solutions during a group speaking task.
02 🧭 Teaching strategies & methodologies
  • Communicative Language Teaching (CLT): meaningful interaction and authentic communication in real-world contexts.
  • Guided discovery (inductive learning): learners notice patterns and discover grammar rules themselves.
  • Scaffolded reading (extensive to intensive): from global understanding (gist) to detailed analysis.
03 🎒 Resources & instructional materials
  • Visual aids: flashcards and infographics of solar panels, deforestation and wind turbines.
  • Reading handout: a 250-word text, “Protecting Our Planet: Small Actions, Big Impact”.
  • Interactive board: dual-column layout (warm-up, presentation, vocabulary, assessment).
  • Worksheets: differentiated fill-in-the-blank sheets and role-play prompt cards.
04 🧱 Lesson stages & presentation (25 minutes)

Stage 4.1 · WH warm-up & hook (5 min). The teacher writes the question on the board: “What are the main causes of environmental pollution in modern cities, and who is responsible?” Students do a 2-minute Think-Pair-Share, then share ideas with the class.

Sub-step 4A · Vocabulary presentation (10 min) — multi-modal, non-translational techniques:

  • Synonyms & antonyms: renewable ≠ finite / exhaustible; deplete = exhaust / drain, ≠ replenish.
  • Flashcards & realia: solar panels and wind turbines to elicit “sustainability”; a dried-up river bed for “drought”.
  • Context clues: “If we cut down trees without planting new ones, natural resources will deplete.”
  • CCQs: conservation — “Does conservation mean destroying nature or protecting it?” (Protecting it.)

Stage 4.3 · Reading integration (10 min).

  • Extensive reading (gist / skimming): read the 250-word text in 2 minutes to answer: “What is the writer’s main message regarding green energy?”
  • Intensive reading (scanning / analysis): re-read for 5 minutes to locate 3 statistics about carbon emissions and underline conditional structures.

Sub-step 4B · Structure presentation — inductive vs deductive:

  • Inductive (guided discovery): the teacher points to two sentences from the text — “If communities recycle plastic, ocean pollution decreases.” / “If governments invest in solar power, we can save money.” — and asks: “What verb form follows ‘If’? What tense is in the second part?”
  • Deductive option (rule first): the teacher highlights the formula If + Present Simple, Subject + Modal (will/can/must) + Base Verb and explains it is used for real, probable future conditions and solutions.
05 🏋️ Guided & independent practice (20 minutes)

Guided practice (10 min · controlled): in pairs, students complete a scaffolded sentence-matching and gap-fill worksheet, matching cause clauses with effect clauses — e.g. “If we (use) ______ renewable energy, we (not deplete) ______ fossil fuels.”

Independent practice (10 min · free production): a group role-play in which students act as a city council proposing 3 eco-solutions. They create a mini-poster using at least 3 target words and 3 conditional sentences to present to the class.

06 📝 Assessment: formative checks & exit ticket (8 minutes)

Formative: the teacher monitors pair and group talk with a checklist, recording accuracy of First Conditionals and vocabulary use.

Summative exit ticket: each student writes on an individual slip: “Write 1 original conditional sentence using the word ‘sustainability’ to propose a solution for ocean pollution.”

07 💬 Constructive feedback strategy (4 minutes)

Delayed board feedback: common errors collected during group work are written anonymously on the board for peer correction.

Incorrect: “If we will plant trees, air becomes clean.” → Corrected: “If we plant trees, air will become clean.”

Positive reinforcement: praise correct use of synonyms and antonyms during student presentations.

08 🏠 Assignment & follow-up homework

Task: write a 100–120 word persuasive proposal paragraph titled “How My School Can Become Sustainable”.

  • Use at least 3 newly learned words (e.g. sustainability, conservation, renewable, deplete).
  • Include at least 2 correctly structured First Conditional sentences.
📋 Section evaluation10 MCQs

Recall the timings, techniques and tasks of the 60-minute model lesson.

⏱️ ~5 min
💡 Model answer & takeaway

Model answer: warm-up (5) → vocabulary (10) → reading (10) → structure → guided practice (10) → independent practice (10) → assessment (8) → feedback (4) → homework.

Takeaway: a good plan moves from hook to input to practice to proof of learning, and every activity serves a stated objective.
